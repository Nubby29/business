# Sales Playbook

## 1. Lead generation

Initial channels:

- Direct outreach
- LinkedIn
- Local business communities
- Referrals
- Useful automation case studies
- Website inbound leads

Do not mass-send generic messages. Each prospect should receive a short message connected to a visible business workflow or problem.

## 2. Discovery conversation

Goal: understand the workflow before proposing technology.

Suggested sequence:

1. What are you doing manually today?
2. How often does it happen?
3. Which tools are involved?
4. Who performs the task?
5. What causes the most frustration or delay?
6. What would the ideal automated process look like?
7. Are there security, approval, or access requirements?
8. Would an Automation Audit help document the workflow and implementation options?

## 3. Qualification

Capture:

- Company
- Contact
- Industry
- Workflow
- Frequency
- Estimated manual time
- Current tools
- Business impact
- Decision maker
- Desired timeline
- Budget range
- Next step

## 4. Proposal

Every proposal should contain:

- Current problem
- Proposed workflow
- Deliverables
- Customer responsibilities
- Timeline
- Price
- Third-party costs
- Support terms
- Acceptance criteria
- Next action

## 5. Follow-up

Suggested sequence:

- Day 0: proposal/message
- Day 2: concise follow-up
- Day 5: useful observation or example
- Day 10: final check-in
- Later: nurture with useful automation content

Respect opt-outs and platform rules.

## 6. Objections

### "We already have software."

Response direction: identify the manual gaps between existing tools rather than replacing software unnecessarily.

### "Automation is too expensive."

Response direction: quantify the current recurring cost in time, delays, and errors, then determine whether a smaller starter automation makes sense.

### "We can do this ourselves."

Response direction: offer an audit or documented workflow design so the customer can evaluate the work before committing to implementation.

### "AI sounds risky."

Response direction: explain exactly where automation is used, what data is handled, what approvals remain human-controlled, and what monitoring/fallbacks exist.

## 7. CRM stages

1. Prospect
2. Contacted
3. Responded
4. Discovery
5. Audit proposed
6. Audit active
7. Implementation proposed
8. Won
9. Delivery
10. Monthly operations
11. Lost / nurture

## 8. Core metrics

Track:

- New prospects
- Reply rate
- Discovery conversations
- Audits sold
- Audit-to-implementation conversion
- Implementations sold
- Average project value
- Monthly recurring revenue
- Delivery hours
- Gross margin
- Customer retention
